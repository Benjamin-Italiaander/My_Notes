# Defaitistisch (defeatist)

> *'Inclined to do nothing more, because one believes all action is pointless anyway.'*

**Defaitistisch** (defeatist) means that someone assumes defeat or failure in advance, and therefore loses the courage or willingness to keep trying.

1. **Meaning:** Inclined to give up because you are convinced you can't win anyway, or that success is no longer possible.
2. **Feeling:** Discouragement, pessimism and resignation.
3. **Synonyms (Dutch):** moedeloos, mismoedig, pessimistisch, berustend — discouraged, dispirited, pessimistic, resigned.

So a defeatist attitude goes further than mere pessimism. A pessimist expects that something will probably end badly; someone who is **defeatist** may conclude from that that **further effort is pointless**.

### Origin of the word

The word **defaitistisch** comes from the French ***défaitisme***, which in turn is related to ***défaite***: *defeat*.

The term arose during the **First World War**, around 1915.

### Historical context

* **France:** The term was used for people who were convinced that France could not win the war against Germany and who therefore thought the fighting should stop or peace should be made.
* **Political weight:** *Défaitisme* soon took on a strongly negative meaning. People could be accused of undermining the morale of the population or the armed forces by constantly emphasising a possible defeat.
* **Wider use:** Later the term was also used outside the military and political context. In Dutch, **defaitisme** now refers more broadly to an attitude in which someone expects to lose from the outset and therefore sees little or no point in further effort.

### In short

**Pessimism:**
*“I think this probably won't work.”*

**Defeatism:**
*“This isn't going to work anyway, so why should we even try?”*

That last one is the core of a **defeatist attitude**.
