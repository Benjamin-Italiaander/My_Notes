# Pyrrhic victory (pyrrusoverwinning)

A **Pyrrhic victory** is a victory that costs so much and causes so much damage that in the end it can hardly be distinguished from a defeat.

In other words: something can look like a victory at first sight, while its consequences turn out to be negative. It is then, for example, a **tactical victory** but at the same time a **strategic defeat**. The success was simply bought at too high a price.

## Strategic meaning

An important strategic consideration is therefore not only **whether you can win a battle**, but above all **what you have left after that victory**.

Reserves play an important role here. Can lost troops, equipment and other resources be replenished quickly enough? If not, even a series of victories can eventually lead to defeat.

Pyrrhic victories can therefore also play a role in a **war of attrition**. One side can deliberately try to inflict losses on the opponent that are harder for the opponent to replace than for itself.

## Origin of the expression

The expression is named after **Pyrrhus**, king of Epirus, who waged war against the Romans in the 3rd century BC.

Pyrrhus won several battles, but lost so many soldiers in the process that his army kept getting weaker. After his victory at **Asculum**, one of his generals is said to have congratulated him on his success.

According to the Greek historian **Plutarch**, Pyrrhus replied roughly:

> *"If we win one more such battle against the Romans, we shall be utterly ruined."*

— *Plutarch, Life of Pyrrhus, 21.9*

That is where the modern meaning of **Pyrrhic victory** comes from: **you win the battle, but pay such a high price for it that you may end up losing the war.**
