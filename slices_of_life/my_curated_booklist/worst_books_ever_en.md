# My Curated List of the Worst Books Ever Written

### 1. The Miracle Morning — Hal Elrod

*One of the worst, and potentially most dangerous, books I have ever read.*

So far, one book stands out above all others: **The Miracle Morning** by Hal Elrod.

Why do I consider this book dangerous?

Because it encourages people to wake up ridiculously early, sometimes at five or six in the morning, as though sacrificing sleep were some magical formula for success, happiness, and personal growth.

But here's the problem: **people need sleep. People need rest. And sleep deprivation is not a path to self-improvement.**

What makes this book particularly troubling is its target audience. People who pick up self-help books are often searching for something more in life. Perhaps they're unhappy, exhausted, struggling, or simply hoping to become better versions of themselves.

And along comes this beautifully packaged, convincingly written story promising that waking up earlier might just transform their lives.

The danger lies in what happens next.

Some readers may start setting their alarms earlier without adjusting their bedtimes. They sacrifice precious hours of sleep, become increasingly exhausted, and convince themselves that this suffering is somehow part of becoming successful.

But chronic sleep deprivation has real consequences. It affects concentration, emotional stability, decision-making, and reaction times. In extreme circumstances, fatigue can contribute to serious accidents, including car crashes.

**Imagine trying to improve your life by following advice that could actually put your life at risk.**

Of course, waking up at five in the morning isn't inherently dangerous. If you go to bed early enough and get sufficient sleep, there's nothing wrong with it. The problem is promoting early rising as a universal recipe for success without giving adequate weight to individual sleep needs.

What bothers me most is the underlying message: that we should squeeze even more productivity out of ourselves, even if it comes at the expense of something as fundamental as rest.

To me, that's not self-improvement. That's self-exploitation disguised as personal development.

**My verdict: I would never recommend this book to anyone.**

If you want to improve your life, perhaps the first miracle you should consider is getting a good night's sleep.

**Be careful what you read. Not every book that promises to improve your life actually will.**
