# Real feelings versus defensive reactions

The difference between **real feelings** and **defensive reactions** that arise from old pain.

## 🌱 1. **Basic emotions (real feelings)**

These are the *original, pure emotions* that belong to a child and that can be triggered again in the present:

### **✔️ Sadness**
The feeling of loss, pain or abandonment.

### **✔️ Fear**
A feeling of being unsafe, powerless or dependent.

### **✔️ Loneliness**
The feeling of being on your own, of not being connected.

### **✔️ Powerlessness/dependence**
The feeling of having no influence and needing someone.

These emotions are *real*, but often too painful to feel consciously — especially when they come back from childhood.

---

# 🔥 2. **Primal pain (the old, stored pain)**

Deep, old childhood feelings are activated again in the present.

This primal pain usually consists of a mix of:

* **abandonment**
* **rejection**
* **feeling unsafe**
* **not being seen**
* **not being good enough**
* **not being loved**

This primal pain is the core of it all: learning to feel again instead of avoiding.

---

# 🛡️ 3. **Defensive emotions (false, protective emotions)**

These are emotions that *protect* you from feeling the real pain.

Three of them are seen most often as defences:

### **❌ Anger**
Anger protects you from feeling sadness, fear or dependence.
*“I don't want to feel this → I get angry.”*

### **❌ Fear as a defence (exaggerated fear)**
Not real fear, but a fear that feels exaggeratedly big.
It serves as a distraction from the primal pain.

### **❌ Numbing reactions (distance)**
For example: feeling shut off, coldness, rationalising, or no longer feeling anything.
This prevents the real pain from surfacing.

---

# ✨ Summary

So there are three important layers of emotion:

1. **Real emotions from the present** (sadness, fear, loneliness, dependence)
2. **Old primal pain from childhood**
3. **Defensive emotions that block feeling** (anger, fear as a defence, numbing)

---

## Anger is not wanting to feel

Anger often arises because we don't want to feel deeper feelings.
Instead of allowing sadness, fear, loneliness or vulnerability, the brain creates anger as a protective layer.

Recognising this helps you shift from reacting to becoming aware — from anger to the real feeling underneath.

*PRI = Past Reality Integration, a form of therapy developed by Ingeborg Bosch.*

# 🌿 A calm explanation of emotions in PRI therapy

In PRI therapy we look gently at what happens inside us.
We discover that emotions don't just appear out of nowhere, but often carry a message.
Some feelings come straight from the moment, while others are actually echoes of our childhood.

When we learn to recognise this difference, space opens up.
Space to feel, to understand, and eventually to live more freely.

---

## 🌱 Real emotions

Real emotions are the feelings that belong to living in the here and now.
They are soft but clear signals from our inner compass.
They tell us what we need, where our boundaries are, and what matters to us.

These real emotions can be:

* sadness
* fear
* loneliness
* the feeling of being dependent or vulnerable

When we allow these feelings, we come closer to ourselves.
We become more honest, more human and more connected.

---

## 🌾 Primal pain

Sometimes, in the present, we touch something that is actually old.
A feeling that was once too big for us as a child — too painful, too overwhelming.

We call this old pain **primal pain**.
It isn't “wrong” or “weak”; it is simply a piece of the past that still lives in us.

Primal pain often consists of:

* abandonment
* rejection
* the feeling of not being seen
* the idea of not being good enough
* feeling unsafe or dependent

When this old pain is touched, our body reacts as if it is happening again.
That can feel confusing.
But it is precisely in recognising it that the possibility of healing lies.

---

## 🛡️ Defence

Because real emotions and primal pain can sometimes feel too sharp, as children we developed ways to protect ourselves.
PRI calls this **defence**.

Defence isn't bad — it once helped us survive.
But as adults it can get in our way.

Defence can look like:

* anger
* exaggerated fear
* keeping your distance, numbing, no longer feeling anything
* worrying or controlling
* rationalising instead of feeling

These reactions are really layers that try to prevent us from having to experience the real pain again.

When we learn to recognise these defences, we no longer have to go along with them.
We can gently ask:
*“Which feeling is this trying to protect?”*

---

## 🌸 Back to gentleness

PRI invites you to reconnect with your real feelings, step by step.
Not by forcing, but by listening.
By slowing down.
By allowing yourself to be honest about what you encounter inside.

When the defences soften and the primal pain may be seen, there is room for healing.
A sense of steadiness, calm and connection — with yourself and with others.
