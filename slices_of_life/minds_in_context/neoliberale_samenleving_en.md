A neoliberal society is one with a strong emphasis on **free markets, competition and individual responsibility**. The idea is that businesses and markets often work more efficiently than the government.

In short, neoliberalism roughly says:

* the government should interfere less with the economy and business
* more things may be privatised
* competition leads to better prices and innovation
* people are largely responsible for their own success

Well-known topics that are often linked to neoliberalism:

* privatisation of healthcare, energy or public transport
* more flexible employment
* fewer social benefits
* lower taxes for companies
* an emphasis on economic growth and efficiency

Historically, it became popular from the 1980s, under leaders such as:

* Margaret Thatcher
* Ronald Reagan

In the Netherlands, some people say that parts of society have become neoliberal through:

* market forces in healthcare
* housing market problems
* flexible (temporary) contracts
* the performance society

But the word is also often used as criticism. Critics argue, for example, that:

* inequality grows
* public services can get worse
* everything revolves too much around money and efficiency
* people experience more stress from constant competition

Supporters, on the other hand, say:

* innovation goes faster
* the economy grows faster
* people get more freedom of choice
* governments become less sluggish and expensive

So a “neoliberal society” is not one exact system, but rather a way of looking at the economy and society in which market forces take centre stage.
