The documentary *The Desert of the Real* is interesting because it doesn't approach psychosis from a purely biological perspective, but also pays attention to personal experiences and social influences.

This raises the question of how much childhood experiences, such as upbringing, attachment, trauma and the social environment, contribute to vulnerability to psychosis later in life.

Although psychosis often has a biological component, research shows that environmental factors can also play an important role. The documentary therefore invites us to think about the interplay between predisposition, childhood and society.

To what extent are psychological vulnerabilities determined by biology, and to what extent are they shaped by the circumstances in which we grow up?
