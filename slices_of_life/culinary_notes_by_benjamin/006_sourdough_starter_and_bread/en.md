# Sourdough Bread
For sourdough bread you need a starter. You can always get a piece of starter from someone who already has one; a culture that has existed for a while is stronger and easier to maintain. But you can also make your own starter. I explain the process briefly below:

---

# Homemade sourdough starter in 1 week

**Make your own natural leavening agent with nothing but flour, water and time.**

## What do you need?

* Organic wholemeal rye flour
* Organic wholemeal wheat flour and white wheat flour (mix 1:3)
* Water (±35°C)
* A clean glass jar & spoon

---

## Day 1 – Getting started

Mix 50 g rye flour with 50 g water. Stir until smooth, cover loosely with a cloth and put it somewhere warm (20–25°C).

## Day 2 – Rest

Leave it alone and check for the first bubbles.

## Day 3 – First feeding

Keep 20 g of starter, add 40 g water and 40 g of the flour mix. Mix well. Keep it warm and mark the level with a rubber band.

## Day 4–7 – Feed daily

Every day:
→ keep 20 g of starter,
→ add 40 g water + 40 g of the flour mix.

The starter becomes more active and airy every day.

## Day 8 – Ready!

Your starter is lively, smells fresh and sour, and doubles in volume: ready to use.
Keep it in the fridge when you are not baking; feed it once a week to keep it active.

---

# Maintenance

Maintain your starter by feeding it once a week with 2 parts water and 3 parts flour. Let it double in volume and then either use it, or put it in the fridge to use the next day / feed again in a week.

**Tip:** feed a young starter every 3–5 days instead.

---

# Method

For this recipe you need **115 grams of active homemade sourdough starter** and **300 grams of water at about 45°C**.

**You also need:** a cast-iron pot and a proofing basket (banneton).

---

### 1.

In a large bowl, mix 300 grams of water at about 45 degrees with 350 grams of white wheat flour and 100 grams of wholemeal wheat flour using a wooden spoon until well combined, about 3 minutes. Cover with a damp tea towel and leave for half an hour to an hour.

### 2.

Mix the active starter into the dough until it is completely absorbed, knead for about 3 minutes, then add the salt. Knead for at least another 3 minutes.
Check whether you can stretch a membrane from the dough (the windowpane test): if you can stretch a small piece so thin that light shines through without it tearing, it's good; if not, knead for a few more minutes.
Wet your hands now and then to stop the dough from sticking.

### 3.

Let the dough rest for 1 hour in a greased bowl covered with a damp tea towel.
After an hour, do a few *stretch and folds*: with wet hands, gently lift the dough, let the front and back stretch out and fold them under the middle.
Turn the dough a quarter turn and repeat.
Is this very easy? Then do it two more times.
Put the dough back in the bowl and let it rest for another hour.

### 4.

Repeat the folding 2 more times, with an hour in between each time.

### 5.

Dust the worktop with a little flour. Carefully lift the dough out of the bowl without pressing out the air bubbles.
Place the dough top side down on the worktop.
Gently stretch it into a square.
Fold the left and right sides to the middle.
Turn a quarter turn.
Fold the left and right sides to the middle again.

### 6.

Turn the dough over onto a clean part of the worktop, so the floured side is on top.
Place your hands behind the dough so your little fingers are under it.
Gently pull the dough towards you across the table, turn it a little and repeat.
This creates a nice round ball of dough (boule) with tension on the surface.

### 7.

Place the dough top side down in a proofing basket and keep it in the fridge for at least 12 and at most 36 hours.

### 8.

Preheat the oven together with the cast-iron pot or Dutch oven to 240°C (464°F).

### 9.

Carefully turn the bread out of the basket onto a sheet of baking paper and score the top a few cm deep with a sharp knife.

### 10.

Carefully take the pot out of the oven, place the bread (with the baking paper) in the pot and add 3 ice cubes.
Put the lid on and return the pot to the oven. Bake for 15 minutes.

### 11.

Take the pot out of the oven, lower the temperature to 200°C (392°F) and take the bread out of the pot.
Place it on a rack and bake for another 15–20 minutes until golden brown and cooked through.

### 12.

Let it cool completely before slicing.
